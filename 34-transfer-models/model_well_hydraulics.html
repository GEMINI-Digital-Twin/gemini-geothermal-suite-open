5.2. Well: Pressure and Temperature Hydraulics

5.2.1. Description

The gemini_model.well.pressure_drop.DPDT model computes the pressure and temperature profile along a multi-section wellbore, for single- or two-phase flow, in either the production (upward) or injection (downward) direction.

The well is discretized into a series of cells (with length, diameter, inclination, and roughness defined per cell). For each cell, the model selects a friction-pressure-drop correlation depending on whether the flow is single-phase liquid or a gas/liquid mixture, and adds a hydrostatic pressure term and a heat-loss-driven temperature change. Results are accumulated along the well to give the total pressure drop and the outlet (wellhead or bottomhole) pressure/temperature.

This model underpins the Production well performance application (VLP calculations) and the ESP model’s inlet/outlet pressure estimates in ESP Performance.

Fluid properties (density, viscosity, gas mass fraction, etc.) required for these calculations are obtained from a PVT model, see Fluid: PVT Properties.

5.2.5. Correlations

For each well cell, DPDT computes the fluid mixture properties with the PVT model (see Fluid: PVT Properties), then selects between two friction/gravity correlations depending on whether a gas phase is present (gemini_model.well.correlation), plus a separate temperature-drop correlation:

Correlation

Used for

DarcyWeisbach

Single-phase friction pressure drop and hydrostatic pressure change, used when no gas phase is present (\(m_g = 0\))

BeggsBrill

Two-phase (gas/liquid) friction and hydrostatic pressure drop, used when a gas phase is present

TemperatureDrop

Along-well temperature change due to heat transfer with the surrounding formation, based on cell heat-transfer coefficient and surface area

5.2.5.1. Darcy-Weisbach (single-phase)

gemini_model.well.correlation.darcyweisbach.DarcyWeisbach computes the friction and gravity pressure-drop terms for single-phase liquid flow, using the Swamee-Jain explicit approximation of the Colebrook-White friction factor.

Reference: Swamee, P. K., & Jain, A. K. (1976). Explicit equations for pipe-flow problems. Journal of the Hydraulics Division, 102(5), 657-664.

\[Re = \frac{|u_{s,l}| \, \rho_l \, D}{\eta_l}\]
\[f = \frac{0.25}{\left[\log_{10}\left(\dfrac{K}{3.7 D} + \dfrac{5.74}{Re^{0.9}}\right)\right]^2}\]
\[\Delta p_{fric} = f \, \frac{\rho_l \, L}{D} \, \frac{|u_{s,l}| \, u_{s,l}}{2} \qquad \Delta p_{grav} = \rho_l \, g \, L \, \sin(\theta)\]

5.2.5.2. Beggs-Brill (two-phase)

gemini_model.well.correlation.beggsbrill.BeggsBrill computes the friction and gravity pressure-drop terms for two-phase (gas/liquid) flow, following the Beggs-Brill correlation as presented in Shoham, O. (2006), Mechanistic Modeling of Gas-Liquid Two-Phase Flow in Pipes, p. 59. Internally, inputs are converted to US field units for the correlation and results are converted back to SI.

Reference: Shoham, O. (2006), Mechanistic Modeling of Gas-Liquid Two-Phase Flow in Pipes, Society of Petroleum Engineers, p. 59.

Step 1 — Flow parameters. The superficial gas/liquid velocities \(u_{s,g}\), \(u_{s,l}\) give the mixture velocity, no-slip liquid holdup, liquid velocity number, and mixture Froude number:

\[u_m = u_{s,l} + u_{s,g} \qquad \lambda_l = \frac{u_{s,l}}{u_m}\]
\[N_{Lv} = 1.938\, u_{s,l} \left(\frac{\rho_l}{\sigma}\right)^{0.25} \qquad Fr_m^2 = \frac{u_m^2}{g \, D}\]

Step 2 — Flow-regime boundaries. Regime boundaries \(L_1\)-\(L_4\) are functions of \(\lambda_l\) only:

\[L_1 = 316\,\lambda_l^{0.302} \qquad L_2 = 0.0009252\,\lambda_l^{-2.4684}\]
\[L_3 = 0.10\,\lambda_l^{-1.4516} \qquad L_4 = 0.5\,\lambda_l^{-6.738}\]

Comparing \(\lambda_l\) and \(Fr_m^2\) against \(L_1\)-\(L_4\) classifies the flow into one of four regimes: segregated, intermittent, distributed, or transition (interpolated between segregated and intermittent).

Step 3 — Liquid holdup. A horizontal-flow holdup \(H_{l,0}\) is first computed with regime-dependent coefficients \((a, b, c)\):

\[H_{l,0} = a\,\frac{\lambda_l^{\,b}}{Fr_m^{2c}}\]

\(H_{l,0}\) is then corrected for pipe inclination:

\[C = \max\!\big(0,\ (1-\lambda_l)\ln(d\,\lambda_l^{\,e}\,N_{Lv}^{\,f}\,Fr_m^{2h})\big)\]
\[\psi = 1 + C\left[\sin(1.8\theta) - \tfrac{1}{3}\sin^3(1.8\theta)\right] \qquad H_l = H_{l,0}\,\psi\]

with regime- and flow-direction-dependent coefficients \((d,e,f,h)\) (distributed flow uses \(\psi = 1\), i.e. no inclination correction; for the transition regime, \(H_l\) is interpolated between the segregated and intermittent values using the same weighting factor as the regime boundaries). \(H_l\) is clamped to \([0, 1]\).

Step 4 — Two-phase friction factor ratio. With \(y = \lambda_l/H_l^2\):

\[\begin{split}s = \begin{cases} \ln(2.2y - 1.2) & 1 < y < 1.2 \\[4pt] \dfrac{\ln y}{-0.0523 + 3.182\ln y - 0.8725(\ln y)^2 + 0.01853(\ln y)^4} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \qquad f_{tp}/f_n = e^s\end{split}\]

Step 5 — Pressure gradients. Mixture properties use the no-slip holdup \(\lambda_l\) for the friction term and the (slip) liquid holdup \(H_l\) for the gravity term:

\[\rho_m = \lambda_l \rho_l + (1-\lambda_l)\rho_g \qquad \eta_m = \lambda_l \eta_l + (1-\lambda_l)\eta_g \qquad Re_m = \frac{u_m \rho_m D}{\eta_m}\]
\[\lambda_{fric} = \left[-0.8685\ln\!\left(\frac{1.964\ln(Re_m) - 3.8215}{Re_m} + \frac{\varepsilon}{3.71 D}\right)\right]^{-2}\]
\[\Delta p_{fric} = \tfrac{1}{2}\rho_m\,u_m|u_m|\;\lambda_{fric}\; (f_{tp}/f_n)\;\frac{L}{D}\]
\[\Delta p_{grav} = \big[H_l\rho_l + (1-H_l)\rho_g\big]\,g\,L\,\sin(\theta)\]

5.2.5.3. Temperature Drop

gemini_model.well.correlation.temperaturedrop.TemperatureDrop computes the along-cell temperature change from heat exchange with the surrounding formation/ambient, using a lumped heat-transfer coefficient and the mixture heat capacity:

\[c_{p,mix} = \frac{m_l}{m_l+m_g}\,c_{p,l} + \frac{m_g}{m_l+m_g}\,c_{p,g}\]
\[\dot{Q} = U \, A \, (T_{in} - T_{ambient}) \qquad \Delta T = \frac{\dot{Q}}{(m_l + m_g)\,c_{p,mix}}\]
